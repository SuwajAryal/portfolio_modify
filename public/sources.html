BUILT WITH THE OPEN-SOURCE COMMUNITY

The people and projects
behind this experience.

The scroll-driven education journey combines community mapping software and real geographic data. The separate pixel-art explorer scenes are original generated artwork selected and requested by the portfolio owner.

Selected explorer scenes

The Himalayas, hills, and Terai illustrations were created as original pixel-art drafts and selected by the owner, then adjusted to give the character slightly fairer skin. Their outer backgrounds were removed so the scenes blend with the lighter page. They use short four-frame loops, with still frames for paused and reduced-motion viewing. These are imaginative landscapes, not real footage, a surveyed river network, or a physical flow model. The avatar is a stylized stand-in rather than a portrait.

A map that follows the story

MapLibre GL JS renders the landscape, street maps, and camera transitions. Its official scroll-to-location example and terrain/hillshade examples guided this integration. MapLibre is BSD-3-Clause licensed.

Map style and geographic data

OpenFreeMap provides the existing Liberty map style and hosted tiles, using OpenMapTiles and data from OpenStreetMap contributors. Their attribution is preserved on the map.

Made with Natural Earth for Nepal’s boundary, regional river lines, and mountain labels, extracted from its public-domain 1:10 million datasets. Source coordinates are preserved.

Mapterhorn provides open elevation tiles for Himalayan relief and hillshading. See its source attribution and data terms. The relief uses modest 1.35× vertical exaggeration for clarity.

The river lines show mapped geography. They are not live discharge or simulated flow, and this generalised dataset omits smaller tributaries. More detailed waterways appear from OpenStreetMap as the map zooms in.

The campus marker is an approximate location within the Pulchowk Campus area. Both degrees share that campus; no degree-specific building or surveyed position is implied.
